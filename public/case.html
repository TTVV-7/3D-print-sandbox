Every tool change flushes the old colour out of the nozzle into a purge tower. Artwork on the back plate changes colour twice and never again, so the tower is a couple of layers tall. Carrying it up the sides changes colour on every layer, and the tower can end up heavier than the case.

Detail

Keeps the walls and a 7 mm rim of back plate around the outline and around every hole, and leaves the middle open. Every dimension that can be wrong is still in it, for about half the filament and none of the purge. Print this one first.

The whole case is five to fifteen seconds of work.

The g-code is the painted case, ready to print and not to be re-sliced — the colours live in the toolpath, which is the whole point. The 3MF keeps the colours: the back plate is cut into inlays, one solid per filament, so your slicer opens one object with a part per colour. The STL is the shape on its own — a single colour, because an STL cannot hold more. Neither carries the purge tower or the tool-change ordering; none of that is geometry. A test fit has neither, because leaving the middle of the back plate unfilled is a thing g-code can say and a solid cannot.
